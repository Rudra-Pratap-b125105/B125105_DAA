Q4 — Minimum Cost to Connect Sticks

Suggested filename: Q4_Minimum_Cost_to_Connect_Sticks.txt
1. Problem understanding

We are given several sticks of different lengths. Connecting two sticks costs an amount equal to the sum of their lengths. After connecting them, they become one longer stick.

The objective is to connect all sticks while minimizing the total cost.

For example, connecting sticks of lengths 2 and 4 costs 6 and produces a stick of length 6. That new stick may be connected with another stick later, incurring another cost.

The key observation is that a stick created early may participate in multiple future connections. Therefore, repeatedly connecting the two smallest available sticks is optimal.
2. Approach

Use a min-heap to efficiently retrieve the two shortest sticks.

At each step:

1. Remove the two smallest lengths.
2. Add their lengths to obtain the new stick.
3. Add that length to the total cost.
4. Insert the new stick into the heap.
5. Repeat until only one stick remains.

Choosing the two smallest sticks first minimizes the contribution of those lengths to later merges. This is the same greedy structure used by Huffman coding.
3. Algorithm

1. Read the number of sticks and their lengths.
2. Insert all lengths into a min-heap.
3. Initialize totalCost to zero.
4. While more than one stick remains:
- Extract the smallest stick.
- Extract the next-smallest stick.
- Calculate their combined length.
- Add the combined length to totalCost.
- Insert the combined length back into the heap.
5. Display the total cost.
4. Pseudocode

MINIMUM-CONNECTION-COST(lengths):
create a min-heap from lengths
totalCost = 0

while heap size > 1:
first = extract minimum
second = extract minimum

combined = first + second
totalCost = totalCost + combined

insert combined into heap

return totalCost

5. Sample input

Number of sticks: 4
Stick lengths: 2 4 3 6

6. Sample output

Connection steps:
Connect 2 and 3 -> Cost = 5
Connect 4 and 5 -> Cost = 9
Connect 6 and 9 -> Cost = 15

Minimum total cost: 29

7. Dry run

Initially, the min-heap contains

\\2,3,4,6\\ 

Step 1: Remove the two smallest sticks, 2 and 3.

2+3=5 

The connection costs 5. Insert the new stick of length 5.

Total cost:

C=5 

Heap:

\\4,5,6\\ 

Step 2: Remove 4 and 5.

4+5=9 

The connection costs 9. Insert the new stick of length 9.

Total cost:

C=5+9=14 

Heap:

\\6,9\\ 

Step 3: Remove 6 and 9.

6+9=15 

The connection costs 15.

Total cost:

C=5+9+15=29 

Only one stick remains, so the process terminates.

The minimum total connection cost is

29 
8. Time complexity derivation

Let n be the number of sticks.

Building a min-heap from all stick lengths takes O(n).

Each connection reduces the number of sticks by one. Therefore, exactly n-1 connections are performed.

Each connection involves:

- Two minimum extractions: 2O(log n)
- One insertion: O(log n)

The total time is

beginaligned T(n)&=O(n)+(n-1)bigl(3O(log n)bigr)\\\ &=O(n)+O(nlog n) endaligned 

Thus,

T(n)=O(nlog n) 
9. Space complexity derivation

The min-heap stores up to n stick lengths.

- Heap storage: O(n)
- A fixed number of temporary variables: O(1)

Therefore,

S(n)=O(n)+O(1) 

Hence,

S(n)=O(n) 

Conclusion: Repeatedly connecting the two smallest sticks using a min-heap produces the minimum total connection cost in O(nlog n) time.
